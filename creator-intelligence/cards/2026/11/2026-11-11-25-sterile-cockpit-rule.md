# A crash banned small talk, hospitals copied it

**The find:** A 1974 Eastern Air Lines crash near Charlotte killed 72 people after investigators found the crew chatting about politics and used cars while the plane sank below a safe altitude. The FAA's 1981 response, the "sterile cockpit rule," bans all non-essential talk below 10,000 feet.
**The surprise:** The rule doesn't target skill or stress — it targets ordinary friendly chatter as the lethal variable, which is why hospitals and businesses have since copied it as blocked-out "no-interruption" windows.
**The idea:** [The sterile cockpit rule](../../../concepts/sterile-cockpit-rule.md) — a disaster caused by casual conversation can produce a rule that bans the conversation itself, not just the distraction it causes.
