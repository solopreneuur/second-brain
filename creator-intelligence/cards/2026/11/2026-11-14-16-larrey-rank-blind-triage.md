# The surgeon who ignored rank

**The find:** Napoleon's chief surgeon Dominique-Jean Larrey invented battlefield triage in 1792, sorting the wounded strictly by medical urgency — a badly hurt enemy soldier or private was treated before a lightly wounded French general, reversing the era's "officers first" norm.
**The surprise:** Removing rank from the queue was the controversial part, not the sorting itself — contemporaries expected status to dictate who got treated, and Larrey's refusal to let it earned him safe passage from enemies at Waterloo.
**The idea:** When a resource is scarce and urgent, sort strictly by the severity of the problem, not the status of who has it — status-based queueing quietly kills the things that matter most ([rank-blind triage](../../../concepts/rank-blind-triage.md)).
