# A loom taught Toyota to stop the line

**The find:** Toyota's andon cord, letting any worker halt the whole assembly line to flag a defect, traces back to founder Sakichi Toyoda's automatic loom from the 1890s–1920s, built to physically stop itself the instant a thread snapped. Amazon now uses the same logic to let support reps pull a defective product off sale storewide.
**The surprise:** One of the most famous lean-management principles in business didn't originate on a factory floor. It came from a loom's mechanical safety switch, built to save thread, not morale.
**The idea:** [Jidoka](../../../concepts/jidoka.md): give the person closest to a defect the authority to stop the whole system immediately, because catching an error early is exponentially cheaper than catching it late.
