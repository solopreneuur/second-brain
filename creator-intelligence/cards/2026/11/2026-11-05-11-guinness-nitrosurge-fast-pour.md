# Guinness secretly learned to pour fast

**The find:** Guinness's famous two-part, 119.5-second pour doesn't change the taste — blind tastings show no difference from a fast pour — and the ritual was invented in the 1960s only to make the new nitrogen-keg system feel consistent with the old cask method. Guinness now sells a home device, Nitrosurge, that fakes the "surge and settle" in under 90 seconds.
**The surprise:** The wait isn't protecting flavor — it's protecting a 60-year-old marketing ritual that Guinness quietly re-engineered to be fast the moment nobody in a pub was watching.
**The idea:** [Manufactured tradition](../../../concepts/manufactured-tradition.md) — a ritual can create perceived value disconnected from the product attribute it's supposedly protecting.
