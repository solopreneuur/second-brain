# Your pill's name is part of the dose

**The find:** In controlled trials, identical placebo pills labeled as a brand-name painkiller relieved as much pain as real ibuprofen; the same pill labeled "generic" delivered roughly 40% less relief. Switching a patient's familiar pill to a new generic with a different shape or color measurably raises dropout from treatment.
**The surprise:** The branding isn't decorating the medicine — the label and the pill's look function as part of the actual treatment, so a color change alone can act like quietly withdrawing some of the dose.
**The idea:** An [expectancy effect](../../../concepts/expectancy-effect.md): belief about a treatment's strength shapes the treatment's measured strength, down to the packaging it arrives in.
