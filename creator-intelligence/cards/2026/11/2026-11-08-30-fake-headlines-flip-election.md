# Fake Headlines Alone Flipped an Election

**The find:** In a study by Pfister, Schwarz and colleagues, participants browsed a fabricated online newspaper where one fictitious candidate's name appeared in about 95% of headlines with zero evaluative content, just the name repeated — in the mock election that followed, 81.8% of participants voted for the frequently-named candidate.
**The surprise:** No policy claims, no endorsement, no likability cues — just repeated exposure to a name in fake headlines was enough to swing roughly 8 in 10 people's votes.
**The idea:** [The mere-exposure effect](../../../concepts/mere-exposure-effect.md): repetition stripped of all content still manufactures preference, the same mechanism behind brand-name familiarity.
