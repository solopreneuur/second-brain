# A crashed bomber built the surgery checklist

**The find:** In 1935 an expert pilot crashed a prototype B-17 bomber after forgetting to release a new control lock. The fix wasn't more training — it was a short index-card checklist, after which the fleet flew 1.8 million miles with zero accidents. Decades later, surgeon Atul Gawande and the Boeing engineer who helped design that checklist built the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, cutting major surgical complications from 11% to 7% across eight hospitals.
**The surprise:** The fix for expert failure under complexity wasn't a smarter expert — it was a piece of paper that experts initially resisted as beneath them.
**The idea:** [Cross-domain transfer](../../../concepts/cross-domain-transfer.md) — checklists beat expertise under complexity and distraction, whether the stakes are a cockpit or an operating table.
