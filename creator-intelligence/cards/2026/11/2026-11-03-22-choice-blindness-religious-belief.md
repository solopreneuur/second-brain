# Swap your answer, keep defending it

**The find:** Researchers had participants rate religious beliefs — the afterlife, whether religion is a force for good — on a slider, then covertly reversed some of their ratings and asked them to justify the "new" answer as their own. A large share of participants, religious and atheist alike, accepted and defended the reversed belief.
**The surprise:** People didn't just fail to notice their own stated belief had been flipped — they built a justification for the opposite view, and then actually reported believing it more afterward.
**The idea:** [Choice blindness](../../../concepts/choice-blindness.md) — the confabulated reason for a choice you didn't make can update the underlying attitude itself.
