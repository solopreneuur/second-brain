# Nokia made paper for a century first

**The find:** Nokia began in 1865 as a wood-pulp paper mill on a Finnish rapid. The modern "Nokia" only took shape through a 1920s merger of that paper mill with a rubber-boot maker and a cable manufacturer, three unrelated industrial firms sharing one brand because they shared the same hydropower source.
**The surprise:** The 1967 merged conglomerate made toilet paper, tires, TVs and cables at the same time, and mobile phones weren't the company's focus until CEO Jorma Ollila divested everything else starting in 1992 — more than a century after the Nokia name first existed.
**The idea:** A brand name can outlive and outgrow the business that created it; a company's "core business" is often just whatever survived the last ruthless divestment.
