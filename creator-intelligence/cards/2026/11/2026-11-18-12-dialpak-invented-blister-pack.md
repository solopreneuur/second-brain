# The dial that invented blister packs

**The find:** Illinois engineer David Wagner built a rotating two-disk pill dispenser in 1962 after watching his wife struggle to track loose birth-control pills against a calendar. Ortho Pharmaceuticals commercialized it as the "Dialpak" in 1963, and its day-labeled, one-pill-per-slot logic became the template for every blister pack pharmacies dispense today.
**The surprise:** A husband solving his wife's daily pill-counting headache with a toy-like snap-fastener design accidentally shaped the layout of nearly every blister pack on a drugstore shelf.
**The idea:** Converting an abstract memory task ("did I take today's dose?") into a physical, visually checkable state — an empty slot — turns compliance into something your eyes verify instead of your memory ([Compliance by geometry](../../../concepts/compliance-by-geometry.md)).
