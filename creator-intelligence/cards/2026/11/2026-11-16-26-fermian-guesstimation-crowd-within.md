# Guessing twice isn't the best way to fix a guess

**The find:** A 2024 study tested "Fermian guesstimation" — breaking one hard estimate into smaller sub-estimates and combining them, the way physicist Enrico Fermi famously approximated unknowns — against the standard trick of asking yourself the same question twice and averaging the two guesses. The Fermi-style breakdown cut error more than simple re-asking did.
**The surprise:** The fix for a bad single guess isn't to doubt it and guess again, it's to stop guessing the target directly and guess things next to it instead.
**The idea:** [Crowd within effect](../../../concepts/crowd-within-effect.md): decomposing a judgment into independent parts manufactures more error-cancelling disagreement with yourself than simply doubting your first answer.
