# A death that capped the medical workweek

**The find:** 18-year-old Libby Zion died in a New York hospital in 1984 after residents who'd been on duty roughly 18-36 hours straight missed a dangerous drug interaction. The case drove the Bell Commission and, eventually, national rules capping medical resident shifts at 24 hours and 80 hours a week.
**The surprise:** The reform wasn't forced by a safety agency or a study — it was driven by a grieving father's newspaper op-ed and a grand jury indictment, public pressure moving a system that internal review hadn't.
**The idea:** [Invisible fatigue failure](../../../concepts/invisible-fatigue-failure.md) — the person making a high-stakes call is often the last to notice they're too impaired to make it well.
