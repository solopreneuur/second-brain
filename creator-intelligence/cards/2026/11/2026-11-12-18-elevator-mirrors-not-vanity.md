# Elevator mirrors aren't there for vanity

**The find:** After post-war skyscrapers generated complaints about slow elevators, building managers began installing mirrors in elevator lobbies and cabs, mainstream by the 1960s and 70s, not for grooming but because giving a waiting person something to look at compresses how long the wait subjectively feels compared to staring at a blank door.
**The surprise:** It's cheaper to hang a mirror than to speed up an elevator motor, so building owners solved a complaint about engineering with a trick about attention instead of fixing the actual machine.
**The idea:** How long a wait feels is a separate variable from how long it actually is, and occupying someone's attention during idle time is often a cheaper fix than shortening the time itself.
