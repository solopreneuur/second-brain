# "Raise it and I'll kill you"

**The find:** When Costco's current CEO Craig Jelinek told founder Jim Sinegal that rising costs meant the $1.50 hot-dog-and-soda combo was losing money, Sinegal told him: "If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out." The combo has stayed $1.50 since 1985.
**The surprise:** Costco didn't just absorb the loss — it rebuilt its supply chain around a joke-sounding threat, bringing hot dog production in-house and switching soda suppliers just to keep one single-digit price frozen for four decades.
**The idea:** A [self-imposed constraint](../../../concepts/self-imposed-constraint.md) companies keep even when it stops making short-term financial sense, because the discipline itself is the asset.

## In depth

Craig Jelinek told this story himself at an April 12, 2018 luncheon for the Issaquah Chamber of Commerce in Washington state, where Costco is headquartered. It was one anecdote inside a broader talk about company operations. Local outlet 425 Business wrote it up the following week, and it sat mostly unnoticed for over two years until September 2020, when a screenshot of a Mental Floss article citing the piece went viral on Twitter. Snopes rated the quote "True" in March 2021, since it's a direct account from the CEO himself.

Jelinek's exact words: "I came to [Sinegal] once and I said, 'Jim, we can't sell this hot dog for a buck fifty. We are losing our rear ends.' And he said, 'If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out.'" The combo — a quarter-pound beef dog, bun, condiments, and a 20-oz fountain drink — is a genuine loss leader; analysts estimate prep costs alone eat most of the $1.50, and Costco now sells well over 100 million of them a year.

"Figure it out" led to real supply-chain moves: Costco stopped buying Hebrew National hot dogs and built its own Kirkland Signature hot dog plant near Los Angeles around 2008–2009, later adding a second near Chicago. It also dropped Coca-Cola for Pepsi in 2013 specifically to protect the price point, and moved to cheaper fountain drinks.

The policy keeps getting reaffirmed. Longtime CFO Richard Galanti said in 2022 the price would stay "forever." When he hedged slightly before retiring in early 2024, saying it was "probably safe for a while," his successor Gary Millerchip used his very first earnings call as CFO to state plainly: "I also want to confirm the $1.50 hot dog price is safe." As of late 2026, it still is — it would be roughly $4.25–4.50 today if it had tracked inflation.
