# Facebook had a name for defrauding kids

**The find:** Unsealed court documents from 2019 showed Facebook internally called it "friendly fraud" when children spent their parents' money on in-game purchases without permission. Between 2008 and 2014, minors racked up more than $34 million in unauthorized Facebook game purchases, and employees were reluctant to add a confirmation step because it would cut revenue.
**The surprise:** The company that built its reputation on removing friction deliberately kept friction out of one specific flow, precisely because friction would have stopped fraud against children.
**The idea:** [Friction](../../../concepts/friction.md): the cost of "frictionless" design is sometimes paid by whoever friction would have protected.
