# "5 days missed" beat "90% attendance" in parent texts

**The find:** Across 108 UK secondary schools, a BIT trial reworded attendance alerts to parents from a percentage ("Tom's attendance was 90%") to a concrete day count ("Tom missed 5 days last term"). Pupils whose parents got the day-count version were absent 0.21 fewer days per pupil over the year — equal to 15 extra attendance days per 100 pupils.
**The surprise:** The effect was largest for the pupils schools most needed to reach — 0.51 fewer days missed for pupils eligible for free school meals, more than double the average.
**The idea:** A concrete count lands harder than the exact same fact expressed as a percentage, because a percentage doesn't picture anything ([Salience nudge](../../../concepts/salience-nudge.md)).
