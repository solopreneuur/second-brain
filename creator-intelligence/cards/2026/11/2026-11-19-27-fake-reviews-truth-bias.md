# Warning people about fake reviews doesn't help

**The find:** University of South Florida researchers told participants in advance that exactly half of 20 restaurant reviews they'd read were fake. Participants still classified an average of 11.38 of the 20 as "real" — and rated negative fake reviews as even more credible than positive ones, despite negative reviews being disproportionately the fake ones in real platform data.
**The surprise:** Forewarning people about the exact base rate of fakes barely helped at all, and the direction people trusted most — negative reviews — was the opposite of where the real fakes actually concentrate.
**The idea:** A truth bias can override explicit, known information about how often you're being lied to, which is why disclosure alone rarely fixes a trust problem the way platforms hope it will.
