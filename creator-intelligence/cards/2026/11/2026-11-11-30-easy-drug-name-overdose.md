# An easy-to-say drug name raised doses

**The find:** Researchers gave people fake drug names that varied only in how easy they were to pronounce. Participants chose higher doses — for themselves and for a hypothetical child — when the name was easy to say.
**The surprise:** A name's syllable-ease alone, with zero pharmacology attached, pushed people toward giving a child more medication.
**The idea:** [Cognitive fluency](../../../concepts/cognitive-fluency.md) — how easily something is processed gets misread as a signal of how safe or true it is, even for a decision as consequential as a dose.
