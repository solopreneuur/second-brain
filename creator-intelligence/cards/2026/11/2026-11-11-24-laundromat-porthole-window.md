# The laundromat window was never security

**The find:** The round glass door on a front-loading washer comes from Bendix's 1937 home automatic washer. The shape is a mechanical necessity — a rotating drum needs a watertight circular seal — but Bendix marketed it as letting housewives enjoy "the thrill of seeing one's laundry going through its motions."
**The surprise:** Everyone assumes the window exists so attendants can watch for theft or problems — it was actually sold as pure entertainment for a brand-new 1930s technology, not security.
**The idea:** [Spectacle as feature](../../../concepts/spectacle-as-feature.md) — a design constraint gets marketed back to customers as a delight, and the retrofitted "practical" reason arrives only later.
