# A brand designed to look cheap

**The find:** After the 1970s oil-crisis recession, French retailer Carrefour launched "produits libres" — plain white packages with black generic-name text, no brand, no slogan, no photo — because it signaled "cheap" better than any design could. Canadian retailer Loblaws took it further, painting its "No Frills" stores entirely black and yellow, deliberately the colour combination least used by any major brand.
**The surprise:** A company spent real design effort inventing a "look of cheapness" — the absence of branding became its own carefully researched marketing asset.
**The idea:** [Anti-branding](../../../concepts/anti-branding.md) — stripping out the usual cues of quality becomes its own unmistakable signal, because it reads as unmanipulated.
