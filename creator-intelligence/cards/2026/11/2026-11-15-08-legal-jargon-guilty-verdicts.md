# Jargon makes jurors vote guilty

**The find:** A 2026 University of Florida study gave 1,092 mock jurors identical trial testimony either in plain language or dense legal jargon ("fraudulent conveyances," "escheatment reporting"); the jargon-exposed jurors convicted more often.
**The surprise:** They were simultaneously less confident in their own verdict and less trusting of the justice system — the mental strain of the jargon got misread as the defendant's guilt.
**The idea:** [Cognitive fluency](../../../concepts/cognitive-fluency.md): how easy information is to process gets mistaken for a judgment about its content.
