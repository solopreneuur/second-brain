# Pianos are tuned to be equally wrong

**The find:** A piano can't be tuned so every interval is mathematically pure — the math doesn't close. So tuners settled on "equal temperament": spreading the unavoidable error evenly across all twelve notes, so every key is equally, imperceptibly out of tune rather than some keys pure and others unplayable.
**The surprise:** The instrument millions of people think of as "in tune" is, by design, uniformly mistuned. Perfection in any one key was sacrificed on purpose so the whole system would work.
**The idea:** [Cross-domain transfer](../../../concepts/cross-domain-transfer.md): when a perfect fit is mathematically impossible, deliberately spreading the imperfection evenly often beats optimizing one part at the expense of the rest.
