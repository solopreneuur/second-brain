# One option alone is a harder sell than two

**The find:** Daniel Mochon's DVD-player experiments found shoppers were consistently less willing to buy a single model shown on its own than the exact same model shown side by side with a competing brand.
**The surprise:** Adding a competitor into the picture should logically only make a product harder to choose — instead, people shown just one option search harder for alternatives and more often walk away buying nothing at all. A rival, even one you don't pick, makes the decision feel safe to make.
**The idea:** Without a comparison point, people don't feel licensed to commit — a named effect called single-option aversion ([Single-option aversion](../../../concepts/single-option-aversion.md)).
