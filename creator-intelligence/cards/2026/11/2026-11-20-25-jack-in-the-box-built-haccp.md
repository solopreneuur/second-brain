# A burger chain's crisis rewired food safety

**The find:** The 1992-93 E. coli outbreak traced to undercooked Jack in the Box hamburgers killed four children and sickened hundreds. The company hired microbiologist David Theno and gave him full authority to install HACCP — a system that monitors the few specific points in a process where failure is catastrophic, rather than inspecting only the finished product. The USDA later mandated it industry-wide.
**The surprise:** Theno's own conclusion wasn't technical — he said food safety failures are "a lack of leadership," meaning the identical checklist produces completely different outcomes depending on whether executives actually enforce what it finds.
**The idea:** A [high-reliability organization](../../../concepts/high-reliability-organization.md) monitors its critical control points, not its finished output — and gives the monitor real authority to act on what they find there.
