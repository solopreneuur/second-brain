# A cave diver's math was built from dead men

**The find:** In 1979, cave explorer Sheck Exley published a handbook built from autopsying real cave-diving deaths. His analysis found a missing gas reserve was a factor in 11 fatalities, so he formalized the "Rule of Thirds": use a third of your air going in, a third coming out, and never touch the final third except as someone else's emergency supply.
**The surprise:** The rule isn't sized for the dive you expect to have, it's sized for the exact failure — a buddy's total gas loss at the farthest point — that kept killing people, decided in advance so panic never gets a vote.
**The idea:** [Precommitment](../../../concepts/precommitment.md): a reserve set by studying past failures beats a reserve felt out in the moment, because the decision gets made before the moment that would corrupt it.
