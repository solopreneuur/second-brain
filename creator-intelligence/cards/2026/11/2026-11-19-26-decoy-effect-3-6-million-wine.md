# The decoy effect tested on 3.6M real sales

**The find:** Researchers tested the classic decoy effect, where an inferior third option makes a target option look better, on 3.6 million real UK supermarket wine purchases from over 755,000 shoppers. Decoys did nudge people toward the target wine, but the real-world lift was only about 1%, and frequent shoppers were immune to it entirely.
**The surprise:** The decoy effect is usually sold as dramatic using lab setups like the Economist subscription trick — the first mega-scale field test of it shows the effect is real, but tiny, and fades out entirely once a customer has enough experience with the category.
**The idea:** An effect that looks large and reliable in a tightly controlled lab study can shrink to nearly nothing once it meets a real market full of repeat customers who've learned the category.
