# Sega paid Nvidia to walk away

**The find:** In 1996, with its NV1 chip failing, Jensen Huang told Sega he could not deliver the console chip it had contracted Nvidia to build — then asked Sega to pay the full $5 million anyway. Sega America's CEO agreed despite getting nothing in return.
**The surprise:** Huang got paid in full for a product he told the client, in advance, he would never deliver — and used the money to rebuild Nvidia from scratch around the industry-standard approach it had previously bet against, producing the RIVA 128, Nvidia's first real hit.
**The idea:** No named concept — a client paying to let a failing supplier go, rather than fight for a refund, because keeping the supplier alive served it more.
