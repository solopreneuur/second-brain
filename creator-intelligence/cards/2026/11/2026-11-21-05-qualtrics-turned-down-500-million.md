# Qualtrics Turned Down $500 Million

**The find:** In 2012, with Qualtrics doing about $50 million in revenue, co-founder Ryan Smith turned down a roughly $500 million acquisition offer from SurveyMonkey.
**The surprise:** A reporter asked him on the record, "You just turned that down?" Six years later SAP bought Qualtrics for $8 billion — 16 times the offer he'd rejected.
**The idea:** [Contrarian conviction](../../../concepts/contrarian-conviction.md) — holding a bet everyone else is certain is a mistake, specifically because the certainty itself is the signal you're onto something they aren't pricing in.

## In depth

In 2012, Qualtrics was a bootstrapped survey-software company in Provo, Utah, pulling in roughly $50 million in annual revenue, run by brothers Ryan and Jared Smith alongside their father Scott and co-founder Stuart Orgill. That year, SurveyMonkey made an offer to acquire Qualtrics for approximately $500 million. Ryan Smith has said he spent about a week going back and forth, pulled between the certainty of a $500 million payout and the instinct to "go big" instead. He turned it down. The family's resistance to selling traced back to their father's scar tissue from the dot-com bust years earlier — Scott Smith, by Ryan's account, would do "nothing" that risked losing control of the company. Rather than sell, Qualtrics took its first outside capital: a $70 million round from Accel and Sequoia, the beginning of a shift from a closely held family business to a venture-backed one. Smith later recalled his wife telling him, after the decision, "You don't need a bunch of money and I don't want you home more."

The rejection became public when Business Insider's Julie Bort, covering the funding round, asked Smith point-blank: "Do you know how much money that is? You just turned that down?" Smith has said the question rattled him — "she scared me, because I had doubts." He later called her back after each subsequent valuation milestone (a $1 billion round, then $2.5 billion in a 2016 Series C) to report on the company's progress.

Qualtrics kept compounding: revenue hit roughly $290 million in 2017 (up 52% year over year) and was on pace to exceed $400 million in 2018, with net retention above 120%. On November 11, 2018, just four days before Qualtrics's scheduled Nasdaq IPO, SAP announced a deal to acquire it outright for $8 billion in cash — about 16 times the 2012 offer Smith had turned down. Ryan Smith stayed on as CEO.
