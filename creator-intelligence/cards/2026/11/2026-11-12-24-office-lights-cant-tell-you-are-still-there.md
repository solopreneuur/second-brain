# Office lights can't tell you're still at your desk

**The find:** Most office occupancy sensors use passive infrared, which detects a change in heat signature, not presence. A person sitting still, reading or typing emits a steady signature with nothing new to detect, so after a timeout window the light cuts out even though the room is occupied, a behavior building codes descending from ASHRAE 90.1-1989 mandate for energy savings.
**The surprise:** The sensor isn't malfunctioning when you have to wave your arms at your desk, it's doing exactly its legally required job, detecting motion, which turns out to be the wrong proxy for a quiet worker.
**The idea:** A policy built around the common case (people move most of the time) can create a bad experience in exactly the case it didn't model, and the cheap fix often isn't worth it to whoever specified the policy.
