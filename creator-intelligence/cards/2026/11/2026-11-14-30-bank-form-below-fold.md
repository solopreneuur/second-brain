# A bank moved its form below the fold

**The find:** First Midwest Bank A/B tested loan landing pages and found the version with the lead form placed below the fold, forcing visitors to scroll past persuasive copy before being asked for info, beat the standard above-the-fold form by 52% in conversions.
**The surprise:** It breaks the most repeated rule in landing-page design ("always put the form above the fold"), and a conservative regional bank, not a scrappy startup, is the one who proved it wrong.
**The idea:** People convert better after being sold, not before — asking for commitment too early can cost more than the "friction" of making them scroll ([sell before you ask](../../../concepts/sell-before-ask.md)).
