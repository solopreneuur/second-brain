# The dairy case with no doors

**The find:** More than half of refrigerated supermarket sections (yogurt, eggs, dairy) have no doors, even though refrigeration is over 50% of a store's energy bill and doors would cut that cost. The open-case design dates to the 1930s and has never been revisited.
**The surprise:** It's not negligence or a dark pattern. It's a 90-year-old frozen assumption about shoppers wanting to handle perishables, kept long after the cost math turned against it.
**The idea:** [Path dependency](../../../concepts/path-dependency.md): legacy design decisions calcify into an invisible industry standard long after their original rationale has shifted.
