# Drive-thru menus are built around eye-tracking data

**The find:** Restaurant trade press details how chains use eye-tracking research to find the "hot spots" on a drive-thru menuboard — eye-level, right-of-center — and reshuffle which items sit there. One major chain's board redesign using this data was credited with a $15 million sales lift.
**The surprise:** The layout that looks like simple signage is actually the output of a deliberate attention-mapping exercise most customers will never know happened, built to steer the eye before it ever reaches the order button.
**The idea:** Physical placement alone, with no change to price or product, can move millions in sales once it's aimed at where attention actually lands ([Salience nudge](../../../concepts/salience-nudge.md)).
