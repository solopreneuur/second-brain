# CIA split on bin Laden's odds

**The find:** Before ordering the bin Laden raid, Obama polled his CIA analysts for a probability the target was really in the Abbottabad compound. Leon Panetta recalls the room split wildly: some said 60%, some below 50% ("too risky"), one analyst said 90%.
**The surprise:** The most consequential national-security decision in a decade was greenlit not on consensus or certainty but on a wide spread of probability estimates that never converged.
**The idea:** High-stakes calls often get made on genuinely disagreeing probability estimates, not resolved certainty — numeric probability, not vague confidence words, is what let decision-makers act despite the spread.
