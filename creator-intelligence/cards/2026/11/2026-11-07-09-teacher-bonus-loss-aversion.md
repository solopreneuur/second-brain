# A bonus teachers could lose doubled scores

**The find:** In a Chicago Heights field experiment, one group of teachers got a standard bonus at year-end tied to their students' gains. Another group got the same expected bonus paid up front, with a contract to return part of it if students underperformed. The "give it back" framing raised math scores by 0.2 to 0.4 standard deviations; the standard year-end bonus produced no measurable effect at all.
**The surprise:** The money on offer was identical. Only the framing changed — earn it later vs. keep it now, only if you deliver — and that framing alone outperformed most tested education interventions.
**The idea:** [Loss aversion](../../../concepts/loss-aversion.md) doesn't need a gamble or a lab choice to work; paying people first and threatening to take money back drives more effort than offering the same money as a future reward.
