# A judo move became a business-school strategy

**The find:** Harvard Business School professors David Yoffie and Mary Kwak named "Judo Strategy" directly after the martial art's core principle: using an opponent's own mass and momentum against them, rather than meeting force with force. They used it to explain how small challengers, like Netscape against Microsoft, beat far larger incumbents through movement and leverage instead of direct confrontation.
**The surprise:** A named, taught competitive-strategy framework isn't a metaphor borrowed loosely from judo, it's an explicit, acknowledged transplant of a specific 19th-century Japanese martial-arts principle into how business schools teach small companies to fight big ones.
**The idea:** An opponent's size and strength can be the exact lever a smaller player uses against them, if the smaller player moves instead of pushing back ([Cross-domain transfer](../../../concepts/cross-domain-transfer.md)).
