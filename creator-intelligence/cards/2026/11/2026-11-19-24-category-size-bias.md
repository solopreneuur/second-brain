# The bigger pile feels luckier

**The find:** Category size bias names the pattern where people judge an outcome as more likely simply because it belongs to a bigger category — bettors value a "black" roulette pocket, one of eighteen, over a "green" pocket, one of only two, even when the individual odds on any single number are identical.
**The surprise:** Your brain substitutes "how big is the group this belongs to" for "what's the actual probability," a shortcut casinos and raffles can quietly exploit by changing how options are grouped rather than their real odds.
**The idea:** Framing choices by category size, not just by probability, shifts how desirable or likely people feel an outcome is, which is why the same odds can be sold as "safer" or "riskier" just by how the groups are drawn ([Category size bias](../../../concepts/category-size-bias.md)).
