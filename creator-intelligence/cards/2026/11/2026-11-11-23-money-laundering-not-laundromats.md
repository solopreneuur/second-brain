# 'Money laundering' isn't about laundromats

**The find:** The term "money laundering" first appears in print in a 1970 Toronto Globe and Mail article, then spreads through 1973 Watergate coverage — decades after Al Capone, who was never charged with laundering money at all.
**The surprise:** The popular story that the term comes from mob-owned laundromats is chronologically impossible: self-service laundromats didn't exist until 1934, three years after Capone's conviction.
**The idea:** [Folk etymology backfill](../../../concepts/folk-etymology-backfill.md) — a plausible-sounding origin story for a term gets invented and spreads after the fact, filling in a gap nobody actually recorded.
