# One trivial choice fixed mystery boxes

**The find:** In a 407-person study, people who felt low personal control bought far fewer "mystery" products than high-control people (purchase-intent scores 4.30 vs 5.16 on a 7-point scale). When researchers simply let low-control shoppers pick among a few mystery-box options, the gap vanished completely (5.24 vs 5.27).
**The surprise:** The fix for uncertainty-aversion wasn't removing the uncertainty — it was adding a trivial, inconsequential choice on top of it.
**The idea:** Giving someone a meaningless pick inside an otherwise random process can neutralize their discomfort with randomness just as effectively as giving them real control ([Illusion of control](../../../concepts/illusion-of-control.md)).
