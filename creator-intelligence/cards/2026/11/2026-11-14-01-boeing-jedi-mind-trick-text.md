# Boeing's "jedi mind trick" text

**The find:** Boeing's chief technical pilot on the 737 MAX, Mark Forkner, texted a colleague "Looks like my jedi mind trick worked again!" after talking FAA regulators out of requiring extra simulator training on MCAS, the flight-control software later tied to two fatal crashes.
**The surprise:** The joke wasn't discovered by an investigator digging for it — Boeing's own lawyers found it in 2019, after 346 people were already dead, and had to hand it over.
**The idea:** Internal bravado, logged casually in a text thread, often reveals a company's real risk tolerance more honestly than any official filing [unfiltered admission](../../../concepts/unfiltered-admission.md).

## In depth

In 2016, Boeing's chief technical pilot for the 737 MAX, Mark Forkner, was tasked with convincing the FAA that pilots transitioning from the older 737 NG didn't need simulator training on MCAS — the Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System, new flight-control software that could push the plane's nose down automatically. Keeping MCAS out of manuals and out of simulator curricula saved airlines money and let Boeing claim the MAX was essentially the same plane as before, requiring only a brief iPad course. On September 15, 2016, Forkner told his deputy, Patrik Gustavsson, over text message that MCAS was now active at far lower speeds than originally designed — "It's running rampant in the sim on me," he wrote — a change Boeing never disclosed to the FAA's flight-standards board. Seven weeks later, on November 3, 2016, after Lion Air (then shopping for the MAX) accepted Boeing's minimal-training pitch, Forkner texted Gustavsson: "Looks like my jedi mind trick worked again!" Those messages sat buried until Boeing's lawyers found them in 2019, after both crashes, and handed them to the FAA and House Transportation Committee — triggering public outrage and a fresh review of the certification process.

Lion Air Flight 610 crashed into the Java Sea on October 29, 2018, killing 189; Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302 crashed on March 10, 2019, killing 157 — 346 dead combined, with MCAS misfiring in both. The FAA grounded every 737 MAX worldwide for 20 months, from March 2019 to November 2020. The Justice Department indicted Forkner in 2021 on four counts of wire fraud; a Fort Worth jury deliberated less than two hours before acquitting him in March 2022, with jurors calling it more a "corporate and regulatory failure" than one man's deception — he remains the only individual ever criminally charged over the MAX. Boeing itself signed a $2.5 billion deferred prosecution agreement in January 2021: a $243.6 million criminal fine, $1.77 billion to airline customers, and $500 million for crash victims' families.
