# Stalin's scientist faked potato yields

**The find:** Soviet agronomist Trofim Lysenko told 500 farms to try his summer-potato-planting method, then published results from only the 50 farms that reported success as proof the method doubled yields — and built three decades of USSR biology policy on claims like "rabbits can become chickens."
**The surprise:** With 500 tries, some successes are guaranteed by chance alone; Lysenko took that statistical inevitability as vindication, and the fabricated "win" shaped a nation's farm policy.
**The idea:** Run enough trials and random success is guaranteed — reporting only the winners turns pure noise into "proof" ([publication bias](../../../concepts/publication-bias.md)).

## In depth

Trofim Lysenko, a self-taught agronomist of peasant stock, rose through Stalin's Soviet Union by promising fast fixes to chronic famine and rejecting Mendelian genetics as "bourgeois" and "fascist." He claimed environment alone could rewrite heredity — wheat could become rye, hornbeam could become hazel, rabbits could become chickens — and pushed techniques like "vernalization" and summer potato planting that he swore would double yields. In reality, vernalization improved output by about 0.4%, not the promised 100%, and reporting was never independently verified: farm managers simply filled in their own production numbers, with no audit of accuracy.

His chief scientific rival was Nikolai Vavilov, a globally respected geneticist who had built the world's largest seed bank, with over 250,000 samples. Vavilov was arrested in 1940 on a collecting expedition in Ukraine, accused of spying for Britain due to his foreign travel and correspondence, and sentenced to death — later commuted to 20 years. He died of starvation in a Saratov prison on January 26, 1943. The bitter irony: during the 900-day Nazi siege of Leningrad, staff at his own institute starved to death guarding his seed collection rather than eat it, preserving it for the future even as its creator starved in a Soviet cell.

Lysenko's doctrine was declared the only correct biology by the Lenin Academy in 1948 and held official power until the mid-1960s; Soviet agricultural and biological science stagnated for a generation, and historians tie Lysenkoism's failures to repeated shortfalls, including a disastrous 1955 potato harvest with over 30% losses in Ukraine and the Russian SFSR. He lost his grip only after patron Nikita Khrushchev was ousted in October 1964, after which Soviet scientists and the press turned on him openly. Today "Lysenkoism" is shorthand in science-policy writing for ideology overriding evidence, invoked in arguments about climate policy, COVID science, and politically convenient data suppression.
