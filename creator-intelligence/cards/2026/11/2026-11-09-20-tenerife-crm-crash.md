# A plane crash rewrote pilot radio

**The find:** After two 747s collided on a foggy Tenerife runway in 1977, killing 583 people partly because a crew misheard an ambiguous "OK... stand by for takeoff," global aviation rewrote radio phraseology so the word "takeoff" could only be spoken at the exact moment of clearance. The disaster also became the founding case for Crew Resource Management training, later borrowed by hospitals as team-communication protocol.
**The surprise:** The deadliest crash in aviation history pivoted on a single ambiguous word, so part of the fix was literally banning that word except in one precise context.
**The idea:** A [high-reliability organization](../../../concepts/high-reliability-organization.md) doesn't just add rules after a disaster — it flattens hierarchy so anyone can challenge an authority figure's reading of a situation.
