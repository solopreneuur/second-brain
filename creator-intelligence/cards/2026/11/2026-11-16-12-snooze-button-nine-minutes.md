# Nine minutes of snooze was a leftover gear

**The find:** In 1956, General Electric-Telechron built the first alarm clock with a snooze button. Engineers couldn't fit a clean 10-minute gear into the clock's existing housing without retooling the whole production line, so they used whatever tooth count landed closest — about nine minutes.
**The surprise:** Nobody chose nine minutes for sleep or convenience; it was a leftover of 1950s gear geometry, and that one factory shortcut became the global default, still hard-coded into phone alarm apps today.
**The idea:** [Path dependency](../../../concepts/path-dependency.md): an arbitrary manufacturing constraint becomes an unquestioned universal standard once everything downstream just copies the original instead of re-deriving it.
