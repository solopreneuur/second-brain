# A letter beat an antibiotics lecture

**The find:** Australia's Chief Medical Officer wrote to the top 30% of antibiotic-prescribing GPs. Adding one line comparing their rate to regional peers nearly tripled the effect of an education-only letter, cutting prescriptions 9.3-12.3% over six months and still 9% a year later.
**The surprise:** Doctors, who should respond to clinical evidence, moved more on learning their rank among colleagues than on being told the medical case against overprescribing.
**The idea:** [Social proof](../../../concepts/social-proof.md) nudges work on experts too, not just consumers, when the comparison is personal and specific.
