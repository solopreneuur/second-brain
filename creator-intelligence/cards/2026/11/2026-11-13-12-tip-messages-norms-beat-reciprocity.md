# "It's customary to tip" beats every other tip message

**The find:** Across 436,969 transactions on a global freelance marketplace, a plain social-norm message ("it's customary to tip") raised new buyers' tipping rate by 11.8 percentage points — beating two versions of a personalized reciprocity appeal ("the seller went out of their way for you") in every condition tested.
**The surprise:** The researchers expected reciprocity to win, since it's the more emotionally loaded appeal. The flat, impersonal statement of what other people typically do outperformed it, with no drop in repeat orders afterward.
**The idea:** Telling people what's normal can move behavior harder than telling them why they personally owe something ([Social proof](../../../concepts/social-proof.md)).
