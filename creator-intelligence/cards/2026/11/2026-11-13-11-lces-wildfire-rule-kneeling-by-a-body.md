# A firefighter wrote his safety rule beside a dead colleague

**The find:** Wildland fire supervisor Paul Gleason created "LCES" — Lookouts, Communications, Escape Routes, Safety Zones — in 1991, after 20 years analyzing fireline deaths, writing it down kneeling beside a colleague killed at the 1990 Dude Fire.
**The surprise:** The rule isn't about fighting the fire better. It's entirely about deciding, before anything goes wrong, exactly how you'll get out and where you'll go — decisions Gleason found crews kept trying to improvise mid-crisis, when it was already too late.
**The idea:** Pre-deciding your exit and your fallback position before you commit to a risky action beats inventing one once you need it ([Precommitment](../../../concepts/precommitment.md)).
