# Japan's corner store pays your bills

**The find:** Since 1987, nearly every utility, tax and insurance bill mailed to a Japanese household carries a barcode payable in cash, 24/7, at any convenience store register — no bank account or app required — because it was cheaper for agencies to route collections through konbini's existing point-of-sale network than to staff their own payment counters.
**The surprise:** The country's most mundane retail chain quietly became default national payment infrastructure, used more for bill-paying than shopping in some areas.
**The idea:** Piggyback infrastructure — the cheapest way to build new infrastructure is often to attach it to a retail network that already has the reach for something else entirely.
