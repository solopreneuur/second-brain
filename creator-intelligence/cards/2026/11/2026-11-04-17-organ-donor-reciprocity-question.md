# One question beat the loss-framed appeal

**The find:** On the "Thank You" page after renewing car tax online, the UK tested 8 messages asking people to join the organ donor register. The line "If you needed an organ transplant, would you have one? If so please help others" added a projected 96,000 extra registrations a year; a version using a crowd photo and "thousands of people register" registered fewer people than no social message at all.
**The surprise:** A question that puts the visitor in the recipient's shoes outperformed every appeal to loss, saving lives, or the crowd, and the only variant that backfired was the one built on the textbook "everyone's doing it" trick.
**The idea:** [Reciprocity](../../../concepts/reciprocity.md) framed as a hypothetical favour can outpull both loss-framing and social proof on the same page, in the same test.
