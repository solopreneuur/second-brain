# Joining the mob paid off, evolutionarily

**The find:** A University of Pennsylvania psychology professor argues humans evolved an appetite for publicly condemning others' moral failings because, historically, joining the mob conferred a real competitive advantage.
**The surprise:** Moral outrage is usually framed as righteous; he reframes it as a selfish evolved strategy with a direct personal payoff.
**The idea:** "I suspect it's because a lot of times when you were part of the mob that was condemning someone else, you benefited... you distract attention for yourself, but also oftentimes you were... your allies [helped] get their stuff as one less competitor."
