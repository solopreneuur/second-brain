# Credit bureaus began as confession booths

**The find:** In 1841, Lewis Tappan's Mercantile Agency, the direct ancestor of today's credit bureaus, had clerks read a stranger's financial reputation aloud from behind a screen so the person asking couldn't see the ledger or who else had been asked about.
**The surprise:** The three-digit number you check on your phone before buying a car started out as a literal booth where a clerk judged your trustworthiness out loud, from gossip collected by a secret network of small-town correspondents — "like a confessional," in the words of the people who ran it.
**The idea:** A system built to look neutral and numeric often began as something opaque, personal and moralizing; the number is a shell that quietly inherited the judgment of the booth it replaced.
