# Why pill bottles are orange

**The find:** Prescription bottles are amber because pharmacopeia standards require light-sensitive-drug containers to transmit less than 10% of light in the 290–450nm band that degrades drug molecules — amber is simply the cheapest pigment that hits that spectral cutoff.
**The surprise:** The "pharmacy orange" everyone recognizes on sight is really a compliance filter tuned to a 160-nanometer window, not a branding decision anyone made.
**The idea:** A regulatory technical threshold, applied identically by every manufacturer for purely functional reasons, can accidentally become a universal visual signal for an entire category ([Regulatory default branding](../../../concepts/regulatory-default-branding.md)).
