# Avianca 52's fatal word choice

**The find:** In 1989, Avianca Flight 52's crew told JFK air traffic control they had a fuel "priority" instead of declaring a fuel "emergency," the word their airline had trained them to use; the plane ran out of fuel and crashed in New York, killing 73.
**The surprise:** The crew wasn't silent — they spoke up repeatedly — but used a soft, hedged word that controllers routinely heard without escalating, so a real crisis sounded like a routine request.
**The idea:** Speaking up isn't enough — the exact words have to be unambiguous enough that the listener is forced to treat the message as urgent ([assertive escalation language](../../../concepts/assertive-escalation-language.md)).
