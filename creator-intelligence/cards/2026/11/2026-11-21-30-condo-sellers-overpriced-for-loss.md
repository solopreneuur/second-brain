# Sellers Who Refused to Take a Loss

**The find:** A study of downtown Boston condo sales through the 1990s found sellers facing a nominal loss versus their original purchase price listed 25-35% above the expected market price, scaled to the size of their loss.
**The surprise:** It worked — they actually sold for 3-18% more than predicted — but their units sat unsold measurably longer, and the effect was roughly twice as strong for owner-occupants as for investors.
**The idea:** [Loss aversion](../../../concepts/loss-aversion.md) — a purely personal, market-irrelevant number (what you originally paid) can override market reality and still move the final price.
