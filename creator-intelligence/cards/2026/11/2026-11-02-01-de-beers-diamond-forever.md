# A diamond tradition invented in 1947

**The find:** In 1947, N.W. Ayer copywriter Frances Gerety wrote "A Diamond Is Forever" for client De Beers, after diamond prices had collapsed and only about 10% of US engagements involved a diamond.
**The surprise:** Within a few decades roughly 80% of US engagements included a diamond, and the copywriter who scripted the "timeless tradition" never married herself.
**The idea:** [Manufactured tradition](../../../concepts/manufactured-tradition.md): an entire category-wide custom can be reverse-engineered by one ad campaign, then enforced by peer pressure as if it had always existed.

## In depth

In 1938, De Beers hired the Philadelphia ad agency N.W. Ayer to fix a demand problem. Diamond prices had crashed in the Depression, mines were producing more stones than the market wanted, and diamonds were not yet the default engagement gift: only about 10% of US engagement rings contained one in 1939. Ayer's research found Americans associated diamonds with old wealth and ostentation, not love. The agency's strategy wasn't to advertise a specific diamond or price, but to sell an idea: that a diamond, not any other gift, was the correct symbol of an engagement, and that its size should track the man's commitment and income.

The campaign worked through more than magazine ads. Ayer planted diamonds on Hollywood stars for public appearances and lobbied studios directly — a 1940 Ayer report to De Beers boasted that it had gotten a film's title changed from "Diamonds Are Dangerous" to "Adventures in Diamonds." By 1946 Ayer ran a weekly "Hollywood Personalities" service feeding 125 newspapers descriptions of the diamonds worn by movie stars, designed, per an Ayer memo, to make "the grocer's wife and the mechanic's wife" want what the star had. Copywriter Frances Gerety, who joined Ayer in 1943 and wrote every De Beers ad for the next 25 years, coined "A Diamond Is Forever" in 1947; it ran the following year. Gerety herself never married.

The "two months' salary" spending guideline wasn't part of the original pitch — Ayer suggested one month's salary in the 1930s and didn't push it to two months until the 1980s, once the habit was already fixed. By 1951, roughly 80% of American engagements involved a diamond.

De Beers' supply-side control has since collapsed: its share of global rough-diamond production fell from roughly 90% in the 1980s to about 29% by 2022, squeezed by rivals and a 1994 US antitrust indictment. Lab-grown diamonds, now over 45% of US engagement ring purchases, have crashed wholesale prices, undercutting the "forever" value promise. Parent company Anglo American has written down De Beers' value by $6.8 billion over 2023–2025.
