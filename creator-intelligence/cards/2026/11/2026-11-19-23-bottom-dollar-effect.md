# Your last dollar poisons the purchase

**The find:** The bottom-dollar effect names a pattern where people transfer the pain of running low on money onto the specific purchase that empties the budget — the final $8 left on a gift card, the last bill from a trip's cash envelope, feels disappointing even when the thing bought is genuinely good.
**The surprise:** It isn't the product that disappoints — it's the shrinking bank balance, retroactively poisoning the memory of a purchase you would otherwise have enjoyed just as much.
**The idea:** Your brain substitutes "how much is left" for "how good was this," which is why the same meal or item can feel worse on the day it exhausts a budget than on any other day ([Bottom-dollar effect](../../../concepts/bottom-dollar-effect.md)).
