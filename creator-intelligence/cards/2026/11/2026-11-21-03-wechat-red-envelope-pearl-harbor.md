# A Red Envelope's Payments Coup

**The find:** Tencent built a digital version of hongbao — the centuries-old custom of gifting cash in red paper envelopes at Lunar New Year — into WeChat in January 2014, letting users send randomized cash splits in group chats.
**The surprise:** About 8 million people linked bank cards within the first 8-10 days, with zero subsidy, flipping China's mobile-payments market so hard that Alibaba's Jack Ma reportedly called it a "Pearl Harbor attack" on Alipay.
**The idea:** A [ritual repurposed as a Trojan horse](../../../concepts/ritual-as-trojan-horse.md) — centuries of cultural habit did the distribution work that no ad budget could buy.

## In depth

Tencent's red-envelope feature shipped inside WeChat during the run-up to the January 31, 2014 Lunar New Year, built in about two weeks by a roughly nine-person internal team. It digitized hongbao, cash traditionally gifted in red paper envelopes mainly to children and relatives at New Year. Two formats existed: a one-to-one envelope with a fixed amount, and a "group grab bag" where a sender picked a total sum and a number of packets, dropped it into a group chat, and the app split the money into random, unequal shares (each share randomized between 0.01 yuan and roughly twice the average of what remained), so amounts ranged from a single cent up to about 200 yuan. Whoever grabbed the largest share was tagged "luckiest draw" and socially expected to send the next envelope — a built-in viral loop. Claiming or sending required linking a bank card.

In the roughly 8-10 days around that Spring Festival, more than 8 million users linked bank cards and exchanged over 40 million envelopes, with no user subsidies paid by Tencent. Before launch, iResearch had Alipay at about 82.6% of China's mobile-payment market versus Tenpay's roughly 10%; Tenpay had held about 4% just before the feature. Jack Ma reportedly called the move a "Pearl Harbor attack" on Alipay's dominance in remarks made around 2014-2015, as Alibaba scrambled to respond with its own red-envelope push. The rivalry escalated into an annual arms race: WeChat reportedly handled roughly 1 billion envelopes over the 2015 New Year's Eve, 8.08 billion on 2016's eve, and 14.2 billion on 2017's eve (46 billion over that six-day holiday, up 43% year-on-year). By Q4 2016, Tenpay's share had risen to about 37%, narrowing Alipay's lead to roughly 54%.
