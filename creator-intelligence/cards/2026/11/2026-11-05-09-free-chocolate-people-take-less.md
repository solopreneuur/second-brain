# Free chocolate, smaller portions

**The find:** At a real table in a Chinese city offering chocolates and masks either "free" or for 0.01 yuan, making items free pulled in nearly twice as many takers (43% vs 21%) — but those free-takers grabbed only 2 chocolates on average, versus almost 7 when the price was a token cent.
**The surprise:** "Free" gets far more people to take something, but makes each person take dramatically less — a social-shame brake kicks in exactly where the price brake switches off.
**The idea:** The [Zero-price effect](../../../concepts/zero-price-effect.md) is usually framed as "free causes overconsumption" — here, social appropriateness norms, not price, govern how much people take once price hits zero.
