# He hid his fortune for 13 years

**The find:** In 1984, Chuck Feeney secretly transferred his entire 38.75% stake in Duty Free Shoppers into a Bermuda-based charitable foundation, keeping it hidden even from his own co-founder and business partner, Robert Miller, for over a decade.
**The surprise:** The secret only surfaced in 1996 when a sale to LVMH, which Miller opposed, threatened to expose it in litigation — one of the two men who built a multibillion-dollar retail empire didn't know, for more than ten years, that his partner no longer owned any part of it.
**The idea:** Giving away a fortune quietly can require more operational secrecy than building it did.

## In depth

In 1960, Chuck Feeney and fellow Cornell Hotel School graduate Robert Warren Miller co-founded Duty Free Shoppers, which grew from selling duty-free liquor to American servicemen in Asia into the world's largest luxury-goods retailer, distributing up to $300 million a year in profits to its partners by the mid-1990s. In 1982, Feeney quietly set up a charitable vehicle, the Atlantic Foundation (later The Atlantic Philanthropies), incorporated in Bermuda specifically to avoid the disclosure requirements a U.S. foundation would face. In 1984, at age 53, he irrevocably transferred his entire 38.75% stake in DFS — then conservatively valued around $500 million — into Atlantic, cutting his own net worth to under $5 million. Lawyer Harvey Dale structured the arrangement and bound everyone who learned of it to secrecy; even Robert Miller did not know his partner no longer personally owned his share of the company they'd built together. In 1988, Forbes still listed Feeney among America's 25 richest people, unaware the money was gone.

The secret held for roughly 13 years. It cracked in 1996 when Feeney decided to sell DFS to Bernard Arnault's LVMH; Miller, furious, opposed the sale, calling DFS "my baby," and the partnership broke apart in acrimony. Knowing a lawsuit would expose that his stake actually belonged to a Bermuda foundation, Feeney preempted it by calling New York Times reporters himself. The January 23, 1997 story, "He Gave Away $600 Million, and No One Knew," outed him. Atlantic ultimately netted $1.63 billion from the LVMH deal.

Feeney spent the next decades promoting "Giving While Living," living in rented apartments, flying coach, wearing a $15 watch, and carrying papers in a plastic bag. Atlantic Philanthropies gave away more than $8 billion before formally dissolving in 2020, funding Cornell, Irish universities, human rights, and public health. Feeney died October 9, 2023, in San Francisco, age 92.
