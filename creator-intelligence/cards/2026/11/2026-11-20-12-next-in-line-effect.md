# Why you forget the toast before yours

**The find:** The next-in-line effect: people reliably fail to recall whatever was said in roughly the nine seconds before their own turn to speak, a phenomenon documented since Malcolm Brenner's 1973 study of people reading words aloud in a circle.
**The surprise:** It's not simple distraction — the anxiety and rehearsal of preparing your own line actively blocks the previous person's words from ever being encoded, so no amount of later prompting brings them back.
**The idea:** The [next-in-line effect](../../../concepts/next-in-line-effect.md) names a specific, universal blind spot: self-focused rehearsal doesn't just distract you, it erases what came immediately before.
