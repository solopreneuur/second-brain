# Rhyming Claims Get Judged as Truer

**The find:** Psychologists took real but unfamiliar aphorisms ("Woes unite foes") and swapped one word to kill the rhyme while keeping the meaning identical ("Woes unite enemies") — the rhyming version was rated 22% more accurate as a description of human behavior, even though both were rated equally easy to understand.
**The surprise:** People don't just find rhymes catchier, they genuinely judge rhyming claims as truer — the same mechanism behind "If the gloves don't fit, you must acquit" working on a jury despite not actually being much of an argument.
**The idea:** [The rhyme-as-reason effect](../../../concepts/rhyme-as-reason-effect.md): the aesthetic fluency of a rhyme gets misread as evidence of its truth.
