# Tesla froze every test drive

**The find:** Days before officially starting as Tesla's president in 2015, Jon McNeill discovered Tesla had promised Wall Street 12,000 cars that quarter but sold only 3,000 six weeks in, because commission-free sales reps had quietly stopped following up on roughly 10,000 uncalled test-drive leads.
**The surprise:** His fix wasn't to chase more leads — it was to freeze all new ones worldwide until every backlogged customer got a callback; orders started arriving from Asia and Europe the very next day.
**The idea:** A broken incentive (no commission, no reason to follow up) can disguise itself perfectly as a demand problem.

4 · Tiny change, big result · Founders · [The Knowledge Project](https://podcastnotes.org/knowledge-project/how-elon-musk-solves-problems-jon-mcneill/)

## In depth

In November 2015, days before his official start as Tesla's president of global sales and service, Jon McNeill — a Bain & Co. alum who'd started and sold six companies, most recently insurance-software firm Enservio — went undercover. He visited eight Tesla stores, test-drove cars at each using eight different email addresses, and waited. A week later, not one store had called him back. He then phoned Tesla's head of sales operations and learned the scope: Tesla had told Wall Street it would sell roughly 12,000 cars that quarter; six weeks in, only 3,000 had sold, and roughly 10,000 people who'd taken test drives in the prior 30 days sat uncontacted. The root cause was structural: Elon Musk had deliberately eliminated sales commissions so Tesla stores wouldn't feel like typical car dealerships. Without commission, reps had no financial reason to chase a lead after the test drive — the job's incentive ended at "get them in the car," not "get the signature."

McNeill's fix, executed before he had any formal authority, was blunt: shut off all new test-drive leads worldwide until every store finished calling back its entire 30-day backlog. The directive rolled out globally within a couple of hours. Orders started coming in from Asia and Europe the next day. McNeill called Musk to explain what he'd done and to apologize for acting without asking first; after a pause, Musk reportedly said, "That was a great decision. You're going to fit in here just fine." McNeill went on to run Tesla's sales, marketing, policy, and service org from 2015 to 2018, during which Tesla's revenue grew from roughly $2 billion to $20 billion, then became COO of Lyft in 2018. He's now CEO of venture studio DVx Ventures and sits on the boards of GM and Lululemon. The story anchors both his 2026 book "The Algorithm" and his appearance on Shane Parrish's "The Knowledge Project," where he also lays out Musk's five-step problem-solving method: question every requirement, delete unnecessary steps, simplify, speed up the cycle, then automate last.
