# The gas pump shutoff has no electronics

**The find:** A small venturi tube near a gas pump nozzle's tip continuously sucks in air as fuel flows. When gas rises high enough in the tank to cover that sensing hole, the airflow stops, a pressure-driven diaphragm snaps shut, and the handle releases — entirely mechanical, with no sensors or wiring anywhere in the system.
**The surprise:** A decades-old, completely electronics-free sensor silently prevents a gas-station explosion risk at every single fill-up, and almost nobody who uses it daily knows it's there or how it works.
**The idea:** The most reliable safety systems are often the ones built so simply that they can't fail electronically, which is also exactly why nobody notices they exist.
