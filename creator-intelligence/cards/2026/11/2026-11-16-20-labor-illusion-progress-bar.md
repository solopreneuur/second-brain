# Watching a search "work" made people trust it more

**The find:** Harvard researchers Ryan Buell and Michael Norton ran five experiments showing people rated an identical result as more valuable when a website visibly "worked" for it — travelers preferred a flight-search tool that narrated its searching process over one that returned the same results instantly.
**The surprise:** Watching a fake progress bar crawl literally makes people trust and value the result more than getting the correct answer immediately — visible effort stands in for quality, even when the effort is theater.
**The idea:** [Labor illusion](../../../concepts/labor-illusion.md): people value a result partly by how hard it visibly looked for something else, not by how good the result actually is.
