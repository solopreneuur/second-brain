# IBM's new CEO cancelled its own breakup

**The find:** In 1993, with IBM losing $8 billion a year, outgoing management, the board and outside consultants were already committed to splitting it into nine standalone "Baby Blue" companies, and managers were lining up to run their own spinoffs. Incoming CEO Lou Gerstner killed the breakup in his first 90 days. By his 2002 exit, IBM's market value had gone from about $29 billion to $168 billion, with 65,000 more employees than when he started.
**The surprise:** The breakup wasn't a rumor — it was already underway — when the new CEO simply said no.
**The idea:** [Path dependency](../../../concepts/path-dependency.md): the "obvious" fix (split the sprawling thing into focused pieces) can destroy the one asset — integration — that customers were actually buying.
