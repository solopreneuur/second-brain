# Rumble strips shrink on purpose to fake acceleration

**The find:** Transverse rumble strips before intersections and work zones are installed in sets with intentionally decreasing spacing between the bumps as you approach, not from wear or manufacturing error. The shrinking gaps make the thumping under the car feel like it's accelerating, which the brain reads as "you're speeding up," prompting drivers to brake even though their actual speed hasn't changed.
**The surprise:** The physical sensation of speeding up that makes you instinctively tap the brake near an intersection can be manufactured entirely by spacing, with no change in your actual velocity at all.
**The idea:** A sequence of identical signals can be made to feel like it's accelerating purely by shrinking the gaps between them, letting a designer trigger a real behavioral response without changing the underlying thing being measured ([Signal encoding](../../../concepts/signal-encoding.md)).
