# Fire hydrant colors are a flow-rate readout

**The find:** Under the NFPA 291 standard, a fire hydrant's bonnet and cap color encode its rated water flow at 20 psi: blue for 1,500+ gallons per minute, green for 1,000-1,499, orange for 500-999, red for under 500, so a fire crew can judge a hydrant's capacity from across the street before connecting a hose.
**The surprise:** The color most people assume is municipal branding or a paint-budget decision is actually a pre-connection capacity gauge, built for a crew under time pressure to read in a glance.
**The idea:** Color-as-instrumentation, encoding a number into a color so a trained reader can skip an entire measurement step.
