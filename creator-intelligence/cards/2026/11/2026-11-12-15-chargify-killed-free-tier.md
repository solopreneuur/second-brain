# A startup killed its own free tier overnight

**The find:** Twelve months into Chargify's freemium billing-software business, fewer than 1% of its 2,500 signed-up customers were paying anything. Instead of tweaking the free tier, the founders killed it outright, emailed all 2,500 customers giving them 45 days to start paying or leave, and roughly doubled entry pricing. TechCrunch covered the move in real time as a misstep, not in hindsight.
**The surprise:** The press criticism wasn't written years later as a "here's what they got wrong" retrospective, it was published the week the decision happened, calling it abrupt and risky. The company kept going anyway and became a profitable, growing SaaS billing platform.
**The idea:** A decision condemned as reckless in the moment, not just in hindsight, can still be the correct read of a business model that quietly wasn't working.
