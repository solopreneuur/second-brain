# Named after a ridiculed doctor

**The find:** Ignaz Semmelweis proved in the 1840s that doctors washing their hands before delivering babies cut maternal deaths dramatically — and was mocked, dismissed by his own peers, and eventually institutionalized for insisting on it. The reflexive rejection of new evidence that contradicts an established belief is now named after him.
**The surprise:** The man who proved handwashing saved lives died discredited for saying so, while the habit he championed took decades to become standard practice.
**The idea:** The Semmelweis reflex — people and institutions reject overwhelming proof on reflex when it contradicts what they already believe, no matter how simple or verifiable the evidence is ([Semmelweis reflex](../../../concepts/semmelweis-reflex.md)).
