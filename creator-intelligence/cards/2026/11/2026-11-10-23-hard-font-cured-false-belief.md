# A hard font cured a false belief

**The find:** Participants played doctor, judging whether a fake drug cured a fake disease across 40 random patient records with no real effect; printed in an easy font, they "saw" a strong cause-effect link that wasn't there, but printed in a hard-to-read font, their false belief dropped significantly.
**The surprise:** Making the same false information harder to read made people more skeptical of a drug that does nothing — the inverse of the usual fluency-equals-truth effect.
**The idea:** [Cognitive fluency](../../../concepts/cognitive-fluency.md), mirrored — disfluency slows processing enough to let scrutiny back in.
