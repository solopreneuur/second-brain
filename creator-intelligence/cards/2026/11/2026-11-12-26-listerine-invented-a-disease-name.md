# Listerine invented a disease to sell mouthwash

**The find:** Listerine launched in 1879 as a general surgical antiseptic, then spent decades marketed as a floor cleaner, a gonorrhea treatment and a dandruff cure. Sales stayed modest until the 1920s, when Lambert Pharmacal started marketing it against "halitosis," an obscure medical term for bad breath that the company's advertising turned into a social fear. Revenue went from about $100,000 to more than $4 million within six years, without changing the product at all.
**The surprise:** The company didn't invent a better product, it invented a disease name and sold the cure for it.
**The idea:** A market can be created by medicalizing an ordinary human experience and giving it a name people are afraid to be caught with.
