# Why old memories feel recent

**The find:** The telescoping effect, named by Census researchers Neter & Waksberg in 1964, is the systematic bias that makes major past events — 9/11, a wedding, a graduation — feel closer in time than they really happened.
**The surprise:** People assume memory just fades and things feel "like forever ago"; telescoping shows the opposite also happens, and it's been well-documented and bidirectional since the 1960s, not a personal quirk.
**The idea:** The [telescoping effect](../../../concepts/telescoping-effect.md).
