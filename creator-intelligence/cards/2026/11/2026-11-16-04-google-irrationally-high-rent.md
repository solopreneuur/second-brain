# Google's own memo called its fee "irrational"

**The find:** An internal Google document surfaced at the 2024 DOJ ad-tech antitrust trial showed Chris LaSala, the company's own global strategy lead for publisher-side ad products, calling Google's roughly 20% AdX take rate — about double what rival ad exchanges charged — "irrationally high rent."
**The surprise:** Google's own executive conceded in writing that the fee wasn't justified by the value AdX provided ("I don't think there is 20% of value in comparing two bids"), only by bundling in AdWords demand publishers couldn't get anywhere else.
**The idea:** [Unfiltered admission](../../../concepts/unfiltered-admission.md): the clearest proof a price is really about leverage, not value, is often sitting in the company's own internal email.

## In depth

During the DOJ's ad-tech antitrust trial against Google, held in the Eastern District of Virginia from September 9 to 27, 2024, before Judge Leonie Brinkema, government attorneys introduced an internal document in which Chris LaSala, Google's former global head of product and strategy for sell-side ad products, had written that Google's AdX ad exchange was extracting an "irrationally high rent" by charging publishers roughly 20% of every ad dollar that moved through its auction — about double the roughly 10% competing exchanges charged. LaSala argued the fee wasn't justified by the service rendered: "I don't think there is 20% of value in comparing two bids," he wrote, adding that AdX "is not providing additional liquidity to the market. It is simply running the auction." He said the fee survived not because of the value of the matching process itself but because AdX bundled in unique demand from AdWords, Google's advertiser platform, which publishers couldn't access through any other exchange. DOJ lawyers used the document as a direct internal admission supporting their theory that Google used advertiser-side dominance to prop up supra-competitive pricing on the publisher side.

On April 17, 2025, Brinkema issued a 115-page ruling finding Google liable for illegally monopolizing two markets — the publisher ad server market and the ad exchange market — while ruling in Google's favor on a separate tying claim. A remedies trial followed in September 2025, where the DOJ and seventeen states asked for AdX's divestiture. Google countered with behavioral remedies only. On September 2, 2026, Brinkema rejected the structural remedies, ruling breakup "neither realistic nor needed," and instead ordered behavioral fixes: interoperability with header-bidding systems, a ban on tying ad-server access to AdX use, data-sharing obligations toward rivals, and a six-year compliance monitor. As of November 2026 the parties are finalizing a joint judgment, and Google has said it will appeal the liability finding.
