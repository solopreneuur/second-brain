# A pay cut hurts more than a raise helps

**The find:** Psychologist Leo Crespi trained rats to run for a food reward, then shifted the amount up or down. Rats switched to a smaller reward slowed down far more than the new, lower amount alone would predict; rats switched to a bigger reward sped up more than expected too.
**The surprise:** Motivation doesn't track the reward's actual size — it tracks the *change* relative to what the animal got used to, which is why a demotion from a generous bonus can crush effort even when the new amount is still objectively good.
**The idea:** The [Crespi effect](../../../concepts/crespi-effect.md): reward history, not reward size, sets the baseline that motivation reacts to.
