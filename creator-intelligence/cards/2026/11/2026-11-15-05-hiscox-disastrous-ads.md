# The insurer's "disastrous" billboards

**The find:** Insurer Hiscox and agency Uncommon Creative Studio built 21 outdoor installations across 17 UK cities deliberately made to look vandalized — a billboard with real water cascading down it, sparking "scorched" wiring, mud-splattered panels — each one dramatizing a real small-business disaster that Hiscox insures against.
**The surprise:** An ad campaign that looks broken on purpose drove a 52% jump in unaided brand awareness and the company's highest-ever annual profit.
**The idea:** A product demo can double as its own ad, if the "flaw" on display is exactly the thing the product fixes.

## In depth

In September 2023, UK insurer Hiscox and agency Uncommon Creative Studio launched "The Most Disastrous Campaign Ever," a brand platform built around 21 bespoke special-build outdoor installations across 17 UK towns and cities — billed by media partners Total Media and Posterscope as the largest deployment of OOH special builds in British advertising history. Rather than CGI trickery, the builds were physically fabricated to look like the posters themselves had gone wrong: a burst-pipe rig at a Westfield pump station sent real water cascading down a billboard face, a site in Glasgow carried scorched wiring with sparking LED effects, Bradford got broken, sagging rigging, and other sites were duplicated, mis-sized, hung upside down, mud-splattered, or ran with a misspelled "Hiscox" logo. A Metro newspaper cover wrap ran blank on the front page, inside pages carried "missed deadline" gags, and the campaign took over WeTransfer and WeWork screens nationwide plus radio spots that cut out or glitched mid-ad.

The joke: each visible "mistake" dramatized a real small-business mishap — fire, flood, cyberattack, theft, data leaks — that Hiscox's SME insurance covers. Creative was led by Uncommon (co-founders Nils Leonard and Lucy Jameson's shop); Fiona Mayo (Hiscox UK marketing director) and Ed Birth (head of brand marketing) fronted the work, which later extended to TV, with ads that appeared to air upside-down, in the wrong language, or with no picture — deliberately cleared with broadcasters at script stage to avoid being pulled as faults. Interim tracking showed unaided awareness up around 17%, branded search up roughly 50%, and sales up about 11%; the fuller, headline results cited in later case studies put the campaign's full-year effect at a 52% jump in unaided brand awareness, 47% growth in brand keyword searches, and 16% sales growth, which Hiscox credits with driving its highest-ever annual profit. The work won The Drum Awards for B2B and was shortlisted across multiple UK media and effectiveness award shows.
