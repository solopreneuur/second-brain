# Google's "very revenue positive" email

**The find:** A 2019 email, surfaced as a trial exhibit in the DOJ's antitrust case against Google, has the head of ads telling Chrome and Search leadership that a new mobile layout change "will be very revenue positive" — the ads team and the search-ranking team coordinating directly on a design change for revenue reasons.
**The surprise:** Google has long maintained that ad sales and search ranking are kept separate inside the company; its own executives' email shows them planning a product change together around how much money it would make.
**The idea:** No named concept — just a hidden reason, confirmed in writing, for why a "quality" design change shipped on an accelerated timeline.

## In depth

In a May 3, 2019 email submitted as a trial exhibit in *US v. Google*, Google's head of ads Jerry Dischler wrote to Chrome executive Anil Sabharwal, copying search chief Ben Gomes, ads-and-search VP Prabhakar Raghavan, and Chrome's Nick Fox and John Maletis: "The Search team is working together with us to accelerate a launch of a new mobile layout by the end of May that will be very revenue positive." The email surfaced during the DOJ's search-monopoly trial before Judge Amit Mehta in Washington, D.C., which ran from September 12 to November 16, 2023, with testimony and documents about this exchange emerging around Dischler's appearance in early October 2023. The note was part of a broader 2019 emergency inside Google: in February that year, Dischler and colleague Shiv Venkataraman had declared an internal "code yellow" after search-query growth and revenue fell behind forecast. Gomes, the longtime search executive, pushed back during that episode, writing that the company was "getting too close to the money" and "too involved with ads for the good of the product and company." Raghavan, who oversaw both ads and later search, noted the shortfall was being patched through "heroic RPM engineering" even as underlying query volume stayed soft. The mobile layout change itself — read as a quality-of-service lever that also happened to be "revenue positive" — was accelerated at the ads team's request, with Sabharwal's Chrome team agreeing to push it forward; Dischler added he didn't want to "poison the culture of the team," which is why, he said, he hadn't pushed harder.

DOJ used the exchange to argue that Google's search-ranking and advertising divisions, which the company has long described publicly as walled off from each other, in fact coordinated directly on changes with revenue implications. Mehta ruled in August 2024 that Google illegally maintained its search monopoly through exclusive default-placement deals; a remedies ruling followed in September 2025, and Google filed its appeal in January 2026.
