# The Beer That Begs Patience

**The find:** Dutch brewer Hertog Jan built a campaign telling drinkers not to drink its Grand Prestige beer fresh — register the bottle online and let it age in a "digital ageing cellar" instead.
**The surprise:** Inverting the one rule every beer brand lives by (drink it fresh, drink it now) helped Hertog Jan overtake Heineken as the Netherlands' best-selling supermarket beer, by roughly €87 million in 2025.
**The idea:** [Deprivation marketing](../../../concepts/deprivation-marketing.md) — telling the customer to wait can raise the product's perceived value more than any taste claim.

## In depth

Hertog Jan, the AB InBev-owned Dutch pilsner brand, built a Cannes-winning campaign called "Don't Drink Hertog Jan" around telling drinkers to do the opposite of category norms: let the beer sit. The mechanic centers on the brand's Grand Prestige line, which replaces the standard "houdbaar tot" (best-before) date with the bottling year, and invites buyers to register each bottle on a website into a "digital ageing cellar" that tracks how the beer's flavor develops the longer it's left unopened — reframing storage as a ritual rather than a defect to avoid. The underlying concept traces to 2017's Grand Prestige repositioning and a 2019 activation called "The Digital Ageing Cellar," built by The Valley and Isobar (now Dentsu Creative); GUT Amsterdam later packaged this work into a case study for Cannes under the "Don't Drink Hertog Jan" name.

Commercially, Hertog Jan first overtook Heineken as the top-selling beer in Dutch supermarkets in 2022, when its sales lead was about €17 million. By 2025 that gap had widened to roughly €87 million, with Hertog Jan's supermarket turnover reaching about €343 million. Reported brand market share rose from 13.6% to 19.4% by 2024, while Heineken's domestic share has slid from around 42% to 38% over the past decade amid a broader 9% drop in A-brand spending in Dutch supermarkets in 2024.

At Cannes Lions 2025, "Don't Drink Hertog Jan" won a Gold Lion in Creative Effectiveness — not the Grand Prix, which went to Apple/TBWA's "Shot on iPhone." The win drew public criticism from Dutch ad-industry figures alleging GUT Amsterdam claimed "idea creation" credit for work it didn't originate, relegating The Valley and Dentsu Creative to "additional agency" status, while GUT's global CEO chaired the jury for that same category. GUT defended its credit; the dispute remains unresolved as of the award.
