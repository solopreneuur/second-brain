# Carlsberg's "Probably Not" ads

**The find:** In 2019 Carlsberg dropped its 46-year slogan "Probably the Best Beer in the World," renamed the campaign "Probably Not," and ran its own customers' cruelest tweets about the beer — "the rancid piss of Satan," "bathwater" — as paid ads.
**The surprise:** Reading out its own worst reviews, instead of hiding them, lifted quality perception and made Carlsberg the UK's fastest-growing major beer brand that year while competitors' volumes fell.
**The idea:** [Reframing](../../../concepts/reframing.md) criticism by saying it first, louder and funnier than critics can, disarms it instead of amplifying it.

## In depth

In April 2019, Carlsberg UK and agency Fold7 retired "Probably the Best Beer in the World," a slogan used since 1973, for a campaign reported at roughly £20m. It opened with ads admitting "We focused on brewing quantity, not quality. We became one of the cheapest, not the best," then moved into a "Mean Tweets"-style video where Carlsberg staff read real negative tweets and reviews about their own beer on camera — lines comparing it to "the rancid piss of Satan," "bathwater your nan died in," camel urine, and toilet disinfectant. A TV ad followed in May 2019 bringing back the brand's Danish "philosopher" character, played by Mads Mikkelsen.

Alongside the admission, Carlsberg reformulated its flagship lager and rebranded it "Carlsberg Danish Pilsner." Master brewer Bhavya Mandanna reworked the recipe for a crisper flavour and distinct hop aroma, keeping the ABV at 3.8%, though the company never disclosed which hops it used.

The reported results, via Marketing Week and WARC: a 15% rise in quality perception, a 20% rise among off-trade shoppers, 5% higher purchase intent, 23% higher recommendation rates, 14% growth in on-trade sales, and 3% volume growth against a 7% category decline among rivals. Marketing Week readers voted it campaign of the year for 2019, and it won Best National Campaign at The Drum's Out of Home Awards and recognition at Eurobest.

The old slogan was never permanently killed — it came back as a supporting line. In 2024, Carlsberg launched a new global campaign, "Do the Best Things Begin With Curiosity? Probably," rolled out across 120 markets, which still nods to the original "probably the best beer" line.
