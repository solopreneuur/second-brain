# iPhone's chime was a CD-burn alert

**The find:** The marimba "ding" every iPhone uses for text messages was composed around 1999 by engineer Kelly Jacklin as the "CD burn complete" chime for SoundJam MP, the Mac MP3 player Apple bought and rebuilt into iTunes.
**The surprise:** Jacklin had no hand in building the iPhone and didn't realize for a while that his old disc-burning sound effect had become the most-heard notification tone on the best-selling consumer product in history.
**The idea:** A tool built for one narrow, forgotten job can get silently repurposed into a brand's most recognized asset — meaning accrues through repetition and context, not original intent ([repurposed signal](../../../concepts/repurposed-signal.md)).

## In depth

In 1999, Apple software engineer and hobbyist musician Kelly Jacklin got a request from Jeff Robbin — who, with Bill Kincaid and Dave Heller, had built a Mac MP3 player called SoundJam MP — to compose a short sound for one specific job: telling the user a CD burn had finished. Jacklin wanted something simple, three or four notes, cheerful, able to "cut through the clutter" without being annoying. He pulled sounds from a Yamaha SW1000XG sound card and auditioned marimbas, kalimbas, harps, kotos, and pizzicato strings before settling on a marimba built from notes in a major-scale pattern. He even wrote a small Lisp program to test different note combinations before picking the final one. He saved the file as "158-marimba.aiff" and it shipped as SoundJam's disc-burn-complete chime.

In 2000, Apple bought SoundJam MP outright, kept Robbin and Kincaid on staff, and in January 2001 relaunched the stripped-down, rebuilt software as iTunes 1.0 — carrying Jacklin's little marimba cue along as a leftover system sound. Jacklin himself moved on to Apple's Pro Apps group rather than the team building the iPhone. So when the iPhone launched in January 2007, he had no hand in choosing its alert tones — and was caught off guard to find his old CD-burn chime, relabeled "Tri-tone" (a name he notes isn't technically accurate, since it isn't three tones in the music-theory sense), installed as the default text-message alert on what would become the best-selling consumer electronics product in history. He's since made peace with being the anonymous author of probably the most-heard four notes on Earth, joking in interviews that he still can't walk through a crowded room without his head snapping toward a stranger's phone. The same sound bank still quietly lives on in macOS as the system's CD-burn and Mail "new message" alerts.
