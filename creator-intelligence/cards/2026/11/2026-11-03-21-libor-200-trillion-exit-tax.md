# Everyone agreed it was broken, nobody could leave

**The find:** LIBOR underpinned roughly $200 trillion in contracts — mortgages, bonds, derivatives. Even after regulators confirmed banks were manipulating it, replacing it with a new benchmark (SOFR) took years of globally coordinated rewiring, because every contract, system and model had been built assuming LIBOR would exist forever.
**The surprise:** Regulators, banks and courts all agreed the standard was corrupt, yet its sheer embeddedness, not its usefulness, is what made it almost impossible to leave.
**The idea:** [Path dependency](../../../concepts/path-dependency.md) — a standard survives on how much has been built on top of it, not on whether it's still correct.
